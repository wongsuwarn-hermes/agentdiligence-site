The buying gap

AI-agent buyers are not short of demos. They are short of defensible evidence.

A demo can show that an agent sounds good. A benchmark can rank generic tasks. Neither tells your team what to automate, what to pilot, and what should stay human.

How it works

We turn your support history into realistic agent tests.

The first evaluation is controlled and practical. We use historical tickets and policy context wherever possible, then run candidate agents under comparable conditions.

1

Your work sample

Tickets, transcripts, policies, macros, SOPs, escalation rules, PoC logs, or service-desk exports.

2

Realistic test cases

Customer situations become clear tests with success requirements, must-not rules, and evidence needs.

3

Candidate-agent runs

Agents run against the same buyer-specific cases wherever access allows, including baselines or challengers.

4

Evidence review

We preserve what happened: transcript, tool use, state checks where possible, incident notes, and interpretation.

5

Decision recommendation

Buy, do not buy, pilot narrowly, require human approval, use for drafting only, or gather more evidence first.

Boundaries

What this is not

  • Not a generic model leaderboard.
  • Not vendor marketing.
  • Not a live-production test by default.
  • Not a claim that an agent is safe.
  • Not a replacement for buyer-specific validation.
Evidence ladder

We are willing to say not proven yet.

Public proof can demonstrate the method. Buyer-specific proof requires your tickets, policies, tools, candidates, and risk threshold.
